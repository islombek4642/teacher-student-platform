SECTION 4

You will hear part of a lecture for history students about a relic in Turenne, a town in southern France.

Okay, in this lecture, we are going to learn the history of Turenne, which is a county in the southern part of France. The history of this area can date back to 2,500 years ago. According to Peter Rocher, a French archaeologist, this place was an important agricultural county. In his findings, there were buried pieces of grain and broken utensils, which were evidence of ancient Romans who traded their pottery for grains with people in Turenne. [Q31: pottery]

However, in the 1500s, a Frenchman found what seemed to be the remains of a castle when he was digging a well. There were bricks and stones that looked like part of a building. [Q32: castle]

In the 17th century, a group of British archaeologists came to Turenne to study the relics. Later, the Swedish government also sent a team. The two teams did a thorough survey of the whole area together. [Q33: survey]

In the north-eastern part of the area, archaeologists found signs of a local market. [Q34] There were stone stands that sellers used to place their commodities, and they even found silver coins, which were the currency back then. [Q35]

About one mile to the east, archaeologists found the remains of a building called Cast. It looked like an old workshop, and archaeologists found tools for making textiles. [Q36]

Situated in the central area was a big round building where there was something like a fountain. The team found buckets near it. This showed that it had been the place where people collected water. [Q37]

What surprised the archaeologists most was that there was an ancient theatre in the north-eastern part. Right next to the theatre, archaeologists unearthed a long stone — from this, they concluded that these stones were from an old bridge on the river. [Q38]

In the south-western area, what seemed to be like a garden was uncovered. There were pots and wooden basins. More importantly, there were two statues of lions just beside the gate. [Q39]

Not far from Dowe House, also in the central area, archaeologists concluded this was a Roman bath. It's where rich people went to enjoy a comfortable and relaxing hot bath. Next to the bath was a sculpture of a woman pouring water into the bath. Across from it, on the other side of the bath pool, people can see the sculpture of the Roman emperor who ruled this area for many years. [Q40]

These discoveries are good material for us to study the history of Turenne and southern France. That is the end of part four.
