Section 2. You will hear the director of a club giving a talk about an ancient game played on ice called curling.

It gives me great pleasure to welcome you to the Montreal Curling Club. So first let's take a look at the game of curling. There are four people in each team. 11The first is called the lead. The next is called the second, the next the third, and the final player functions as the captain but is always called the skip.

The area of play on the ice, whether on a rink or a frozen lake, 12is called the sheet. This long thin area is 42.15 metres by only 4.25 metres. At either end of this are the two zones for the targets. 13This one is known as a house.

A little about the equipment. The stones are made from granite. The other distinctive implement is the brush or broom, used to clean the ice and to warm it. You can use your own brush. There are two main types, Scottish and Canadian. The 14former are normally made out of horse hair, and the latter are made of synthetic materials.

Apart from stones and brushes, the only other piece of equipment you would need as a beginner is a 15rubber sole on one of your shoes, depending on which shoe you lead from. The captain will often carry a 16stopwatch to gauge when the ice needs to be changed.

A quick word about Montreal Curling Club. It's renowned — not only because it often wins championships, but 17because it was the first club for any sport in the whole of the North American continent (B — oldest sports club).

It's now well established that curling started in Scotland at least as early as the 16th century. Queen Victoria's patronage attracted interest, and this was enhanced when she granted a royal title to 18the Caledonian Curling Club, which had been set up in the early 19th century to create standard rules for the game (C — fix regulations).

In the 16th century, the Scottish Parliament banned golf and football; local men should spend free time practising archery for self-defence. But 19curling wasn't included in the ban. The art of throwing stones was thought to be just as useful as shooting arrows (B — good training for battle).

Back to Canada. Because of the difficulty in importing granite stones from Scotland, 20cast iron was used instead, obtained from melted-down cannon balls (C — cast iron). Now Canada is regarded as the most successful curling nation in the world.

Section 4. You will hear a lecturer talking on the topic of sustainability.

The term sustainability is not new. It was first coined in 1987 in a report for the United Nations World Commission on Environment and Development. My specific focus: I want to analyse what seems to me to be the 31confusion that surrounds sustainability.

That UN document defines sustainable development as development that allows both the present and all future 32generations to meet their needs.

Here is the first myth. Sustainability is not simply about the environment. In fact, 33the original definition says nothing about [the environment] at all. The original focus was on finding ways to help poor nations catch up with richer nations, which primarily meant giving them similar 34rights to natural resources — water, food, and energy. The ultimate goal was improving 35living standards for all.

Another myth is that sustainability is a synonym for green. The lobby for sustainability acknowledge that the situation is desperate. 36The main problem they state correctly is time, with 6 billion people on the planet and a billion more expected in the next 30 years.

Electric cars, wind turbines and solar cells are key examples. 37Nuclear power, too, is something the sustainability lobby has come to accept, unlike most greens.

And here's the third myth — the role of technology. Take electric cars. New technology is being developed to make better batteries, but a better way of thinking is perhaps to have 38battery stations for drivers to use on roads. When the battery is low, they swap it for a fully charged one — new business thinking, not new technology.

There are similarities in agriculture. Some crops are very thirsty. So many farmers who believe in sustainability are now 39planting crops like sunflowers and wheat, instead of corn, which are happy with much less water.

In terms of water consumption, one of the biggest problems is that our 40diet is changing. As people become richer, they aspire to a diet with more meat.
