Section 4. You will hear a talk to psychology students about people's sense of smell.

It's often said that our ability to sense smells is much less developed than that of animals such as the dog, or a fish such as the shark, or, of course, the creature which is believed to have the most acute sense of smell of all, the 31bear.

The power that smell has over us is, by and large, not something we are aware of. One possible reason is that 32our noses aren't well equipped to detect the source of an odour, and so we dismiss it.

The power of smell will be no news to estate agents. They often suggest that people who want to sell a house arrange things so that there is a smell of newly baked 33bread on the property to encourage viewers to buy it.

Dutch researchers carried out a study in a nightclub. When the aroma of 34oranges was pumped into the venue, the guests partied harder and rated their night more enjoyable.

Another study found that the hint of a smell of lemon-scented detergent was enough to persuade students to 35clean the communal kitchen after using it.

A study this year found that subjecting people to a new smell led people to rely on their 36emotions to make a decision rather than relying on logic, and they performed worse.

Scientists took sweat samples from people doing a skydive for the first time. They also took samples from the same people after they had worked up their sweat in a 37gym, a situation where no fear is involved. The subjects reacted much more strongly to the skydive sweat.

Normally, people pay more attention to angry faces because they pose a 38threat. However, after smelling the fear sweat, participants gave the same attention to neutral expressions.

Some psychologists have been trying to train people's noses. They persuaded 32 students to wear blindfolds and ear defenders and see whether they could sniff out a trail of 39chocolate. After three days of practice, they were much more accurate.

Further evidence comes from a chef who lost her sense of smell after an illness. She trained herself gradually by sniffing 40spices for 10 minutes a day, and her ability to smell returned.
