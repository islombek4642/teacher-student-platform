Section 4. You will hear a university lecturer talking to his students about an architectural studies assignment.

Your assignment is on public monuments — any structure or statue that is built to remind us of important events or of historic figures. At the beginning of Section 1, you should identify the monument you're examining and say why you've chosen it. 31Include a comment on whether you think the monument works. By that, I mean its success.

Next, you should also include in this first section information about where the monument was built, its geographical site, and 32also a discussion of its social importance, how relevant it was for the people of the time. You should also include information on when it was built, its historical background, and make sure you illustrate the assignment appropriately. 33Insert any plans you can find, and make sure any photographs you use have captions.

In Section 2 you'll need to give a detailed account of the design and construction. Firstly, 34I'll want to read about any particular influence you think is apparent in the design. More often than not, there will be something in it that links it to an established style. Secondly, 35I want information about the choice of materials — not only those which were used, but reasons why they may have been selected. Next, 36I'll want mention of any particular skills that were necessary in the construction.

The third section is the most important one. To add to your own comments, you'll need to carry out a survey. Firstly, 37was the reason for erecting the monument a valid one? Was it a waste, or does it represent a sound cultural investment? 38Then, think about its emotional impact. When someone looks at it, how do they feel? And don't forget to 39consider how well the monument sits in its environment. Does it harmonise with it, or does it clash with it?

Oh, and remember, it is important to weigh up carefully how to make the maximum impact within the number of words allowable. You don't get extra marks for lots of factual information. 40You get those for a well-constructed analysis.
